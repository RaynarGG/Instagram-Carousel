PSYCHOLOGY.

52 STUDENTS AND

ONE PLAIN BLACK IKEA BOX

Half the students got the box flat-packed with the instructions and had to build it. The other half got it already assembled. Then everyone was asked what they would pay for it, in a way where lowballing and overbidding both cost them.

PSYCHOLOGY.

BUILDERS BID $0.78.

THE REST BID $0.48.

Same box. Same product. Whoever assembled it paid a 63% premium, and liked it more: 3.81 vs 2.50 on a seven-point scale. They did not just value their work. They valued the box.

PSYCHOLOGY.

EVEN AMATEUR ORIGAMI

FELT LIKE A TREASURE

106 people folded a crane or a frog. Builders priced their own at $0.23; strangers offered $0.05, nearly five times less. Yet builders valued their folds almost like the expert-made ones: $0.27.

PSYCHOLOGY.

IT ONLY WORKS IF YOU FINISH

BECAUSE IT PROVES YOU CAN

A third group stopped two steps early, every piece in hand: they bid $0.59. Finishers bid $1.46, more than double. The authors' read: a finished creation is proof of your competence, and that is what people pay extra for.
