PSYCHOLOGY.

32 STUDENTS AND

A TUB OF ICE WATER

Trial one: a hand in 14 °C water for 60 seconds. Trial two: the other hand, the same 60 seconds, then 30 more while the water was warmed by one degree. Afterwards they had to pick one trial to repeat.

PSYCHOLOGY.

22 OF 32 CHOSE

THE LONGER TRIAL

The long trial held all the pain of the short one, plus 30 seconds more. Anyone minimizing pain would pick the short one: that share should have been zero. It was 69%.

PSYCHOLOGY.

THEY EVEN REMEMBERED IT

AS THE EASIER ONE

Most said the long trial caused less discomfort and was less cold at its worst. Both wrong. Six even reported it was the shorter one. One student muttered: "The choice I made doesn't seem to make much sense."

PSYCHOLOGY.

MEMORY KEEPS TWO MOMENTS:

THE WORST AND THE END

The authors call it the peak-and-end pattern: duration barely counts. In both trials the worst moment was equally bad, but the long one ended warmer. Of those who felt relief at the end, 81% chose it.
