PSYCHOLOGY.

A STRANGER BEGS FOR HELP

OVER THE INTERCOM

Headphones on, a student listens to strangers talk about college stress. Then one of them has a seizure and begs for help. No experimenter is around. Does she get up?

PSYCHOLOGY.

85% HELPED WHEN ALONE

ONLY 31% WITH 4 OTHERS

Alone with the victim, 85% reported the seizure before it ended. Those who believed four others were listening: 31%. In the end, every lone listener reported it. Only 62% of the crowd did.

PSYCHOLOGY.

THE ONES WHO STAYED

WERE MORE NERVOUS

Afterwards they asked: "He's all right, isn't he?" Many had trembling hands and sweating palms. If anything, they were more aroused than the helpers. They did not feel nothing. They were stuck.

PSYCHOLOGY.

THE MORE WITNESSES

THE LESS RESPONSIBLE YOU FEEL

The authors call it diffusion of responsibility: with others around, everyone assumes someone else will act. Alienation, need for approval, personality tests did not predict who helped. The number of bystanders did.
