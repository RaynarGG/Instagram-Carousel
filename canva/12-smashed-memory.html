PSYCHOLOGY.

STUDENTS WATCH A CAR CRASH

IN A LECTURE HALL

150 students watch the same film of a car crash. Each gets one question about the speed, identical except for one verb. A week later, they return. What do they remember?

PSYCHOLOGY.

SMASHED MEANT 32%

HIT MEANT 14%

Asked how fast the cars were going when they smashed into each other, 32% later said they saw broken glass. With hit: 14%. No speed question: 12%. There was no broken glass in the film.

PSYCHOLOGY.

ONE VERB MOVED THE CARS

FROM 31.8 TO 40.8 MPH

In the first experiment, 45 students saw seven short traffic films. Smashed got the highest speed estimate. Contacted got the lowest. Same films. Different verb.

PSYCHOLOGY.

MEMORY ISN'T A RECORDING

IT GETS REBUILT

The authors' reading: questions asked after an event can reconstruct the memory of it. The verb added high speed to the picture, and broken glass fits high speed. The effect ran partly through the speed estimate.
