PSYCHOLOGY.

A BORING HOUR.

THEN A FAVOR.

Students spend an hour on mind-numbing tasks. Then they are asked to tell the next person it was fun. Some get $1. Some get $20. Who ends up liking it?

PSYCHOLOGY.

PAID $1: RATED IT +1.35

PAID $20: RATED IT −0.05

On a scale from −5 (dull) to +5 (enjoyable), the $1 group rated the tasks +1.35. The $20 group: −0.05. A control group that never said a word: −0.45.

PSYCHOLOGY.

$20 EXPLAINED IT.

$1 DIDN'T.

With $20, saying it was fun made sense: they did it for the money. With $1, there was hardly a reason, so their own opinion moved toward what they had said. The authors call the tension cognitive dissonance.
